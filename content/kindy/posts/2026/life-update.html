Content warning Depression, burnout

It's been a while since my last post, which coincides with the beginning of a burnout. It officially started in February, and it's still something I'm recovering from.

So here's a fresh start, one that takes courage, because I really didn't want to write about this. However the writing was on the wall as they say.

The initial embers of this burnout already happened in 2018, I was trying to chase a salary increase that I knew I deserved. Definitely in hindsight looking at my career path, I can concur that that was correct. Another thing that contributed was an hour-long commute each way, with rush hour trains, to get to the office where most days were typical "could have done this at home" days. The straw that broke the camel's back this time, was the lack of autonomy, not being heard and a sheer lack for quality. The fact that they use "decomm'ed" as a verb ("decommissioned") willy-nilly, and that there's even been projects that were already decommissioned while still in development, should tell you enough..

I was already talking with the company doctor and after a few months in April and May I was back at work, then in June there was a bit of a relapse, I had another talk with her and that actually gave me hope; I was under the impression that my recovery would be relatively linear, but she assured me that's not the case, it has ups and downs. Then there was one day where I physically couldn't eat and I knew I had to act. This was now a depression.

I told my general practitioner that I've always been able to function relatively well, even though I knew I had some kind of depression. I was prescribed some medication, with the advice that the first two weeks might feel worse. However the opposite happened for me, basically instantly I started noticing a difference. My sleep improved, I could fall asleep within 30 minutes instead of at least an hour, I noticed during driving that I was more relaxed and forgiving. I started noticing that yawning somehow is a sign for me that I'm releasing stress. (Which otherwise I probably don't know I'm enduring) Basically, I was shown a life that I didn't know was possible, and those first two weeks felt euphoric.

After that it kinda became 'the new normal'. I was told this from the onset, so I knew this medication would lessen the downs, but not automatically give you ups. For that part actual therapy is necessary. I'm definitely noticing that I can feel more grateful for a lot of things, or perhaps not feeling so pessimistic, which would have hidden the underlying feeling.

In general I've had a hard time with feeling emotions, expressing them and even accepting them. My manager and I tried several times to come up with a plan to improve my situation, but after the third time I said "No, let's start inviting the company doctor and mark this as burnout". Not because I thought that was what I had, but because there was no noticable improvement. In my first call with the doctor, she validated everything immediately, told me I had burnout for real and what we could do to fix it, including taking a step back and re-integrating slowly. I felt heard.

So while this is all just early days, I feel quite positive about the future. In the back of my mind that was always the case, but I was drowning in bad thoughts. I'm seeing this as quite a good learning moment, something that I needed to go through, and I'm actually feeling stronger; and as such decided to write about it.

Now the plan is to start picking up more things here on this website, next blog should be about my holiday in Normandy, France.
