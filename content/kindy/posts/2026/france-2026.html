After researching camping gear for most of the early months of the year, we made our decision and planned a holiday trip to Normandy, France.

We chose Normandy because we really wanted to go to France, but not too far. Last time when we took the kids to Wuppertal, it was a 3-hour ride. Normandy was 7 hours, 8 if you included the stops, which we really had to do every 2 hours. It was pretty do-able, but I think if I'm doing Austria or the south of France, I'll probably do night driving so they can sleep most of the way.

The camping we chose was one where my wife went as kid and she had fond memories of it. They had upgraded a lot since those decades and apart from the nostalgia, it actually did look like a great camping for kids.

A really nice swimming pool, with several slides, a lazy river and a very shallow pool with a lot of slides, blocks, fountains for the little ones. They had several playgrounds, an arcade (which we didn't really use, but they still loved it), and two bouncy castles.

It also had a tiny supermarket and a bakery where you could order bread and pastries for the next morning. The sanitation was decent, but not great.

While we also chose this area beacuse of the sea, we really only went once. It was a bit overcast and only 24℃ degrees. It was actually too cold for the 4-year-old. The unfortunate thing is that you also have to park, it's just too far to walk with young kids. On top of that it has to compete against the swimming pool with all of its amenities.

On one of the days we tried to do a little hike in the area of Beuvron-en-Auge, it was called something like the Chapel Tour. It turned out to be a bit much for us as a family, but we did visit the lovely town of Beuvron-en-Auge, as well as visited some chapels by car instead. In France they seem to have 'flower system' given to towns as a rating, and this one scored well, deservingly so. (See tourist information)

On another day we travelled to La Roche d'Oëtre, a very scenic viewpoint in the middle of the forest. We had a picnic at the creek and went for an icecream at the nearby cafe.

On the way back we took a scenic route past Caen and some WWII memorials. We even visited the Pegasus bridge, but that was a complete coincidence.

We visited the historic, touristic town of Honfleur. It's indeed a lovely town, but very crowded and too touristy for my taste. We were trying to find a parking spot, but we had our roof box on the car so we wouldn't fit in 2-meter spots. Google Maps also clearly didn't mind sending us down very narrow lanes, in what is basically the old town. Eventually we found a side street that would require a bit of walking, but we had already made it so far, so it had to do.

The drive home also went well. Kids were happy, snacks were eaten, drinks were picked at the gasstation. I feel like we could have done two weeks instead of one, but it was our first time tent camping, so we just took it all easy.

We had great weather to be honest, two days were overcast, one day was slightly rainy, but in general it was sunny and around 25℃ degrees the entire time.
