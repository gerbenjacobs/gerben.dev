The reason why is that I really like a) the idea of money as the game's main resource, and b) the idea that you can play the game in different playstyles.

What is Wardogs?
If you don't know, Wardogs is a King Of The Hill style game where 3 teams fight over a circle in the middle of the map. The playstyles that I mentioned are basically a fighter, a builder, logistics (trucks or helis). Each playstyle gives you money and XP in different ways, so you can earn money by driving a logistic truck back and forth without ever shooting a gun.

So when I tried to turn that idea into a PBBG, I thought about how to make a real-time game work in a slow and asynchronous way. That's relatively hard, specifically for the fighting portion.
I want to offer the feeling that you need some teamwork, that every type of job gives you money, and that there's some sort of competition, like the 3 factions in #Wardogs.

The initial shift that started to make it work, was to focus the game on PVE, this works very well in a PBBG setting, this of course takes away the competition, but that's why I decided to have the teams somehow score points on top of their individual contributions. The final spark came from the looting in Wardogs and the different types of ammo (9mm, 5.45mm, 7.62mm, etc.).

Zombie Town 🧟

So defense, logistics, PVE, multiple clusters of players; the perfect ingredients for a zombie apocalypse survival game.

Zombie Town is a working title and many of the assumptions I'll mention here are still up for debate and calibration.

In Zombie Town you and 7 other players can start a town (or a compound like in The Walking Dead), where you have to build defenses, grow food and gear up for exploration and looting sprees. You do most of the work during the day, because at night these specific zombies seem to become more aggressive and will raid your base.

You're not the only ones though, there's several other towns with 8 people each also fighting to make their mark in this apocalyptic world.

Communication & Cooperation
The game will revolve heavily around communication, you'll need to figure out who will do what. Not everyone can sit in the guardtower to shoot zombies, or be the chosen gardener that develops a green thumb. Of course you're allowed to switch to another role, but this requires cooperation.

Turns & Actions
Because the game is asynchronous, should be seen as slow and tactical, and understands that people work 40-hours a week, we have to design the game in a way that people can carefully consider their turns and actions.
The idea is that there's a turn every hour, but each turn can consist of 20 actions. People have to create their actionlist and submit it before each hour. There will be options to repeat a list (and now that I'm writing this, maybe also to save lists, so you can easily pick them again).
The question that's still open in my mind, what happens at night; literally the actual night if people are sleeping (which in a game doesn't really exist because of timezones) and the night phase in the game itself, when zombies are raiding bases. Does it make sense to have the game's day night cycle be every 12 hours? Meaning there will be overlap for every timezone around the world to experience both phases. And for example, what will a gardener do at night, will they also man the watchtowers or will they heal people using herbs and medications?

Autonomy
I want the player to feel a lot of autonomy, they can give themselves roles, they can maybe elect a leader (not sure about this yet), they can rename the town etc. They should be free to layout their town however they will; while it's maybe a bit too graphical, it would be kinda nice if they had a grid system where they could physically place objects (guardtower, gate, garden, bunkbeds).

Scavenging
One of the biggest tropes in survival games is scavenging for resources, which is definitely I want to have for my game as well. This is also where the different types of ammunitions can come into play. Sometimes you find ammo that none of your townspeople use, but maybe you bring it home anyway, in fact, maybe you can barter with another group's town?
Maybe scavenging has different types of risk and you need to decide how many people you bring, how many resources (food, water, guns, ammo) per trip. The more you take, the less risky it becomes, but it also can mean you lose a lot when things go sour.
